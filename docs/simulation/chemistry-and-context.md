# Chemistry and Context

## Team Chemistry

Chemistry represents how effectively the roster functions together beyond raw talent.

Potential inputs:

- roster continuity
- coach stability
- scheme familiarity
- leadership
- role satisfaction
- recent winning / losing
- personality compatibility
- contract disputes
- position-group cohesion
- veteran presence
- major roster turnover

Chemistry should operate as a modest bounded modifier. It should matter, but it should not allow a weak roster to consistently dominate a substantially stronger one.

## Position-Group Chemistry

Some cohesion effects may be localized:

- offensive line continuity
- quarterback / receiver familiarity
- secondary communication
- front-seven assignment discipline

This allows roster churn to have football-specific consequences without making the entire team equally affected.

## Home-Field Advantage

Home-field advantage should be modeled as a small contextual modifier affecting areas such as:

- communication
- offensive efficiency
- false-start / procedural error probability
- defensive energy or pressure
- kicking environment where appropriate

The exact magnitude must be calibrated through simulation rather than hard-coded from intuition.

## Environmental Context

Future context variables can include:

- weather
- temperature
- wind
- precipitation
- travel distance
- short rest
- bye weeks
- playing surface
- altitude

Environmental effects should interact with team construction. For example, high wind may reduce the expected value of deep passing rather than applying a generic penalty to both teams.

## Injuries and Fatigue

Player availability and effective performance should reflect:

- injury severity
- durability
- recovery state
- accumulated workload
- short-rest games
- depth-chart quality

Depth therefore becomes strategically valuable even when starters remain the primary determinant of roster strength.

## Seasonal Variance

Players and teams should experience controlled performance variation across a season. This creates hot/cold stretches and uncertainty without permanently changing underlying ability.

Seasonal variance must be distinguishable from true development or decline so the engine does not incorrectly convert temporary performance into permanent ratings changes.
